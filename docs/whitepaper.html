Blaque Baux · White Paper

Harvesting risk, not return

A governed systematic platform built on an uncomfortable finding: at the horizons and instruments tested, there was no exploitable edge in predicting returns. The edge that survives out-of-sample lives in the risk structure.

by Carter Warrens · Blaque Baux · 2026 · reference implementation in Julia
Abstract We describe a risk-premium harvesting strategy — the spine — that allocates by risk rather than return forecast: an inverse-volatility / equal-risk-contribution base blended with a 12-month time-series-momentum trend sleeve, each volatility-targeted on its own realized P&L, scaled by a drawdown regime brake, and rebalanced daily. On 2007–2026 (net of costs) it returns a Sharpe of ≈ 0.97 at a ≈ 11% maximum drawdown, with the trend sleeve carrying the book through 2022. We motivate the design from a direct measurement — candidate return-prediction signals tested at ≈ 50% accuracy (a coin flip) — and we quantify, rather than hide, the strategy's ceiling: this is single-digit-CAGR, ~1.0-Sharpe money, and leverage to reach double digits buys proportional drawdown and is net-negative at today's financing rates.

1The thesis

Most systematic strategies are sold on their ability to forecast. We tested that ability directly. On 60 days of 15-minute data across ~60 S&P names, two candidate alpha models — a three-factor cross-sectional model and a Bayesian Kalman/shrinkage engine — scored 50.9% and 50.7% directional accuracy, with information coefficients of −0.004 and +0.001. Both are coin flips.

So we stopped trying to predict returns and built around what is measurable and persistent: the compensation paid for bearing risk others avoid — the diversification premium, and the trend (crisis-alpha) premium. Covariance is more forecastable than mean; the strategy trades on it.

2Construction

The book is two sleeves. Both are sized by an exponentially-weighted covariance estimate Σ̂ (RiskMetrics, half-life 21), never by a return forecast.

5Governance, and honesty as a feature

Every order passes one governed path enforcing hard invariants — idempotency, per-pool budget and daily-loss limits, data-staleness, position reconciliation, a kill switch, and full fill lineage — beneath a pre-trade safety gate (drawdown and loss halts, gross-leverage and per-name caps). No language model is anywhere near the order path; the strategy is reproducible code.

Just as important is what we do not claim. The Bayesian alpha engine, the Monte-Carlo simulator, and a Sharpe-on-historical-means optimizer are all preserved but archived: the first measured as noise, the second is a risk calculator (it sizes a tail; it does not create return), and the third is the Markowitz error-maximization trap. Sophisticated machinery packages and sizes a signal; it does not create one. That distinction is the whole paper.
