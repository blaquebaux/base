The complete method

The full body of work behind a single-strategy platform: every approach explored, validated, or rejected, and the discipline that decided. Twenty-plus strategies, one honest scorecard, and the reason only a few of them will ship.

by Carter Warrens · 2026 · reference implementation in Julia · figures are historical backtests and paper results on ETF and equity data
The program in one paragraph Blaque Baux began as an alpha-prediction system and became a risk-premium harvester. The reason: rigorous out-of-sample testing found no exploitable predictive edge at the horizons and instruments we can access. We reproduced that null result a dozen ways. Cross-sectional factors, Bayesian filtering, 15-minute signals, cross-industry lead-lag, and pairs arbitrage were all coin flips. What survives is risk structure: diversification and trend premia, harvested by a governed daily process. Around that core we ran a deep second program on convexity and tail protection, on correlation structure, and on aggression and leverage, each ending in a durable, often counter-intuitive law. This paper is the map of all of it: what shipped, what is in live testing, what was kept as research, and, just as prominently, what was tested and rejected, with the reason. The honesty is the point. A strategy is only as trustworthy as the ideas it was willing to kill.

1The founding finding

On 60-day 15-minute data (about 1,547 bars across 60 S&P names), two independent alpha models scored 50.9% and 50.7% directional accuracy. Both were coin flips, with an information coefficient near zero. We then reproduced the null across blue-chip and mid-cap universes, cross-industry lead-lag, and pairs mean-reversion. The conclusion was not "try harder"; it was structural. At retail-accessible horizons and costs, the market has already priced what is knowable. So the platform stopped forecasting returns and started harvesting the premia paid for bearing risk (diversification, trend, term and asset premia), where an out-of-sample edge genuinely survives.

3What's live, and what's graduating

Production: the Spine, a two-sleeve book (inverse-vol/ERC base plus 12-month time-series-momentum trend). Each sleeve is volatility-targeted on its own realized P&L, blended 50/50, and scaled by a drawdown regime brake, then rebalanced once daily and routed through a governed execution layer. Net of costs it delivers a Sharpe near 1.0 at about 11% maximum drawdown, with the trend sleeve making money in 2022 while a long book bled. It now trades six assets (SPY, IEF, TLT, GLD, DBC, DBA).

In live A/B (three isolated paper accounts): single-sign (the control), split-universe, and single-multi-horizon. The two candidate upgrades are measured head-to-head against production on real fills, each with its own state, ledger, and safety files. Nothing graduates to funded capital until it clears out-of-sample and purged cross-validation.

4The research themes

Return prediction: the null result, reproduced

Every route to forecasting returns landed in the same place. The two original alpha engines scored 50.9% and 50.7% directional accuracy on 15-minute data, a coin flip with information coefficient near zero. Blue-chip 12-month momentum managed only 52.1% with a t-statistic of 1.78, below the significance bar of 2.0; mid-cap momentum was outright negative at 48.3%. Cross-industry read-through was priced the same day (correlation 0.7 to 0.9) with next-day predictability near zero, and negative in tech, where chasing a Microsoft or Google earnings pop returned −1.1% against a +0.4% baseline. A diversified pairs-arbitrage book netted 0.15 Sharpe after costs, indistinguishable from noise. Five independent methods, one answer. That consistency is the real evidence: at horizons and costs a retail participant can reach, the market has already priced what is knowable, and it prices it instantly. So we stopped forecasting and built around the risk premia that survive.

Convexity and tail hedging: free vs paid

The deepest thread. Convexity comes in two forms. Free convexity (trend and momentum, funded by the momentum premium) protects sustained crises and improves return. Paid convexity (long volatility or short carry, funded by decay) protects sharp crashes but bleeds and cannot be timed. A paid overlay hurts the Spine, which already owns free convexity; making the trend sleeve multi-horizon is the win instead. The best standalone hedge is diversified across crisis regimes: deflation (VIXY, short-credit) and inflation (short-duration, long-commodities) are anti-correlated, so a small sliver has no blind spot.

Correlation structure: real, but priced

Industries, supply chains, and firms are strongly correlated (sectors about 0.62, big banks about 0.87), but the read-through is instantaneous, which makes it a risk tool rather than an alpha source. And correlation is proportional to shared forced exposure: prudential regulation (banks, utilities) or a common input (airlines share one fuel) homogenizes firms into one factor, while product regulation with idiosyncratic outcomes (pharma and biotech under the FDA) leaves them uncorrelated despite heavy oversight.

Aggression and leverage: the growth-vs-ruin frontier

"Most aggressive" has a principled ceiling (Kelly) and a brutal catch: aggression multiplies edge, and ours is modest. At retail financing, leverage lowers growth; a max-aggressive convex bet has a median terminal wealth below the stake. Rational aggression is fractional Kelly on real edge, or a small barbell sleeve, never all-in.

The archived alpha track

The original Bayesian alpha engine, a Monte-Carlo risk simulator, a Sharpe-on-historical-means "Oracle," and the Gamma-ARMA regime framework are preserved but archived. The mathematics is sound; the measured edge was not there. Sophisticated machinery packages and sizes a signal. It does not create one.

5The durable laws

The findings that survive the small-sample caveats. Directional and structural, they are the real output of the program.

I. At tradeable horizons there is no exploitable return-prediction edge, so harvest premia rather than forecast.
II. Convexity is either free (trend, for sustained crises) or paid (long-vol, for sharp crises). No instrument gives cheap, timed, crash-day protection.
III. Timing the tail removes the tail. Every rule that trims a hedge's bleed also removes its payoff.
IV. Diversification is necessary but not sufficient. An uncorrelated stream only helps if it is positive-expectancy.
V. A hedge's safe sleeve must be inert in a crisis. Anything with hidden crisis-beta is not safe.
VI. There are two crisis regimes, deflation and inflation. They are anti-correlated and need opposite hedges.
VII. Aggression multiplies edge. With none, it multiplies only ruin (Kelly is about 1× here).
VIII. Correlation is real and priced instantly. It is a risk tool, not an alpha source.
IX. Correlation is proportional to shared forced exposure (prudential regulation or a common input); idiosyncratic outcomes decorrelate.
X. The discipline, validate out-of-sample then deploy, is the actual product. A strategy is only as trustworthy as the ideas it killed.

6Governance: the discipline is the product

Every order passes one governed path enforcing hard, tested invariants: idempotency, per-pool budget and daily-loss limits, data-staleness, position reconciliation, a kill switch, and full fill lineage, all beneath a pre-trade safety gate (drawdown and loss halts, gross-leverage and per-name caps, account and data checks). No language model is anywhere near the order path; the strategy is reproducible code. The autonomous daily job runs deterministically pre-open with morning catch-up fires, and reports its book and P&L to a comparison log. Nothing reaches capital without clearing the same out-of-sample bar the Spine did, which is why most of the scorecard is red. That is a feature.

7Honest caveats

Backtests are historical or paper results on ETF and equity proxies, net of modeled costs, and are not indicative of future results. Tail and earnings findings are especially sample-dependent, because the window holds a single sharp crash (COVID) and a single inflation bear (2022). Several positive research results (inverse carry, short-duration hedges) lean on that 2022 window and should be read as shapes, not promises. The validated core is single-digit-CAGR, roughly 1.0-Sharpe money. There is no double-digit-return-at-low-risk configuration, and this program says so plainly.
